Japanese Curry

Yield: 3-4 servings
Prep Time: 15 minutes
Cook Time: 30-40 minutes
Total Time: 45-55 minutes
The sweet onions and aromatic ginger create a savory foundation, while the rich curry roux delivers deep umami notes that coat tender vegetables and chicken. Each spoonful offers comforting warmth balanced by subtle sweetness from the carrots and potatoes.

This classic Japanese curry embodies the comfort food essence of yoshoku (Western-influenced Japanese cuisine) that became popular in Japan during the Meiji era. Unlike Indian curries, Japanese curry features a thicker, gravy-like consistency with a mild, slightly sweet flavor profile that appeals to all ages. The Kinokuniya curry roux, a blend of spices, flour, and oil pressed into convenient blocks, makes this dish remarkably simple to prepare while delivering authentic flavor. The long-simmered vegetables become tender and sweet, creating a hearty one-pot meal that's even better the next day as the flavors deepen overnight.

INGREDIENTS
  • 450 g (1 lb) chicken thighs (or breast), cut into bite-sized pieces
  • 2 tablespoons vegetable oil
  • 2 large onions, thinly sliced
  • 2 medium carrots, peeled and cut into chunks
  • 2 medium potatoes, peeled and cut into chunks
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tablespoon grated ginger
  • 500 ml (2 cups) water or stock (chicken or vegetable)
  • 120 g (1 packet) Kinokuniya curry roux
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce (optional, for extra depth)
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • Cooked Japanese short-grain rice, for serving
EQUIPMENT
  • Large pot or Dutch oven
  • Chef's knife and cutting board
  • Wooden spoon or spatula
  • Grater (for ginger)
  • Ladle
PREPARATION
Prepare Ingredients (10 min)
Cut the 450 g chicken into bite-sized pieces, about 1-inch cubes. Thinly slice the 2 large onions, peel and cut the 2 carrots and 2 potatoes into chunks roughly the same size as the chicken for even cooking. Mince the 2 cloves garlic and grate 1 tablespoon fresh ginger using a fine grater.
Brown Chicken and Onions (8 min)
Heat 2 tablespoons vegetable oil in a large pot over medium heat until it shimmers. Add the chicken pieces and cook, stirring occasionally, until lightly browned on the surfaces, about 3-4 minutes—they don't need to be fully cooked through. Add the sliced onions and continue cooking until they soften and turn translucent, about 4-5 minutes, stirring to prevent sticking.
Add Vegetables and Aromatics (3 min)
Add the carrot and potato chunks, minced garlic, and grated ginger to the pot. Stir-fry for 2-3 minutes until everything is well coated in the oil and the kitchen smells fragrant from the aromatics. This step builds the flavor foundation of your curry.
Simmer Until Tender (20 min)
Pour in 500 ml (2 cups) water or stock and increase heat to bring to a boil. As it comes to a boil, you may see some foam rise to the surface—skim this off with a spoon for a clearer sauce. Once boiling, reduce heat to maintain a gentle simmer. Cook uncovered for 15-20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the vegetables are tender when pierced with a fork and the chicken is cooked through. The liquid should reduce slightly.
Dissolve Curry Roux (2 min)
Turn off the heat completely—this is important to prevent the roux from clumping. Break the 120 g curry roux block into individual pieces and add them to the pot. Stir continuously until the roux completely dissolves into the liquid, creating a smooth mixture. Be patient; it should take about 2 minutes of stirring.
Final Simmer and Season (10 min)
Turn the heat back to low and simmer the curry slowly for 5-10 minutes, stirring occasionally to prevent sticking on the bottom. The sauce will thicken to a rich, gravy-like consistency that coats the back of your spoon. If you prefer a thicker curry, continue simmering a few minutes longer. Taste and adjust seasoning—add 1 tablespoon soy sauce for deeper umami, or season with salt and pepper as needed. The curry should taste rich and balanced, not too salty.
Serve (2 min)
Serve the curry hot over steaming Japanese short-grain rice. Traditionally, the curry is ladled over one side of the rice, leaving some white rice visible. For extra spice, offer cayenne pepper or chili oil on the side. Remember that this curry tastes even better the next day after the flavors have had time to meld—store leftovers in the refrigerator for up to 3 days.
Optional: Sweet Variation
For a sweeter, more complex curry, add 1 grated Honeycrisp or Fuji apple (about 1 cup) along with the carrots and potatoes in Step 3, or stir in 2-3 tablespoons raisins during the final simmer in Step 6. The apple will break down into the sauce, adding natural sweetness and depth, while raisins provide bursts of concentrated sweetness throughout. These additions are popular in many Japanese households and create a more nuanced flavor profile.
