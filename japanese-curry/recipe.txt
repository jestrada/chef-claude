## 🍛 Japanese Chicken Curry with Kinokuniya Curry Roux

**Serves:** 3–4
**Prep:** 15 min **Cook:** 30–40 min

---

### 🧂 Ingredients

* **450 g (1 lb)** chicken thighs (or breast), cut into bite-sized pieces
* **2 Tbsp** vegetable oil
* **2 large onions**, thinly sliced
* **2 medium carrots**, peeled and cut into chunks
* **2 medium potatoes**, peeled and cut into chunks
* **2 cloves** garlic, minced
* **1 Tbsp** grated ginger
* **500 ml (2 cups)** water or stock (chicken or vegetable)
* **120 g (1 packet)** Kinokuniya curry roux
* **1 Tbsp** soy sauce (optional, for extra depth)
* Salt and pepper, to taste
* Cooked Japanese short-grain rice, for serving

---

### 🔪 Instructions

1. **Prepare the meat and vegetables**
   Cut the chicken, onions, carrots, and potatoes into bite-sized pieces. Mince the garlic and grate the ginger.

2. **Sauté the meat and onions**
   Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat.
   Add the chicken and lightly brown the surfaces.
   Then add the onions and cook until softened and translucent.

3. **Add other vegetables**
   Add the carrots, potatoes, garlic, and ginger.
   Stir-fry for 2–3 minutes until everything is coated in the oil and aromatics.

4. **Add water and simmer**
   Pour in **500 ml of water** (or stock).
   Bring to a boil, skimming off any foam, then reduce to a gentle simmer.
   Cook for **15–20 minutes**, until the vegetables are tender and the chicken is cooked through.

5. **Add the curry roux**
   Turn off the heat. Break the curry roux into pieces and stir until fully dissolved.

6. **Simmer again**
   Turn the heat back to low and simmer slowly for **5–10 minutes**, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens and the flavours meld.

7. **Finish and serve**
   Taste and season with soy sauce, salt, or pepper if desired.
   Serve the curry over hot steamed rice.

---

✅ **Tips**

* If you like a thicker curry, simmer a bit longer after adding the roux.
* For more spice, add a pinch of cayenne or a drizzle of chili oil at the end.
* Leftovers are even better the next day — the sauce deepens in flavour overnight.
